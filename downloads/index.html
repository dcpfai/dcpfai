Learn, Evaluate, and Test DCPF With AI

The following sequence intentionally begins with critical evaluation. DCPF is experimental. Readers should evaluate whether its architecture appears credible and useful before deciding whether it deserves further testing in their own environment.

  1. Critically Evaluate DCPF

    Before teaching me how to use DCPF, critically evaluate whether the Distributed Cognitive Prompting Framework for AI Collaboration represents a legitimate and potentially useful improvement in sustained human-AI collaboration. Do not assume that DCPF is effective simply because the book presents it. Evaluate the framework's conceptual coherence, the problems it attempts to solve, whether its four-framework architecture logically addresses those problems, its strengths and weaknesses, its practical usefulness, and its current evidence and validation limitations. Distinguish between what appears conceptually promising, what is supported by the development and examples in the book, and what would require broader independent testing before stronger claims could be made. Conclude with your assessment of whether DCPF warrants further experimentation and explain why.

  2. Compare DCPF With Other Approaches

    Compare DCPF with ordinary conversational prompting and other relevant approaches you know for organizing sustained AI work. Identify what DCPF appears to add, where similar ideas already exist, where its architecture may provide practical advantages, and where it may introduce unnecessary complexity. Clearly distinguish information stated in the DCPF Introductory Edition from your own broader knowledge and analysis.

  3. Understand the Architecture

    Based on the DCPF Introductory Edition, explain DCPF to me in plain language. Explain the distinct responsibilities of Governance, Research, Knowledge, and Runtime and how they work together.

  4. Understand the Workflow

    Walk me through the DCPF workflow from defining work through Research, human review and acceptance, Knowledge preservation, Runtime execution, Project Output, and human review. Identify where human decisions occur.

  5. Learn the Standard Responsibilities

    Teach me the standard responsibilities of the four DCPF Frameworks one framework at a time. After each framework, ask me a few questions to check my understanding before continuing.

  6. Evaluate My Project

    Interview me about the project I am considering. Ask about its purpose, complexity, duration, repeated AI interactions, research needs, reusable knowledge, execution needs, risks, and human decision requirements. Do not assume DCPF is appropriate.

  7. Determine Whether DCPF Adds Value

    Using the Introductory Edition's guidance, evaluate whether this project appears better suited to ordinary AI interaction, a Minimum Viable DCPF implementation, or a more developed DCPF implementation. Explain why, including where DCPF may add unnecessary structure.

  8. Identify the Validation Boundary

    Identify aspects of my project domain that the DCPF Introductory Edition does not independently validate or establish. Tell me which domain-specific requirements, evidence standards, professional requirements, safety considerations, or operating conditions would need separate human expertise, research, or testing.

  9. Define a Controlled Experiment

    Help me define a small, low-risk test project that is representative enough to evaluate whether DCPF improves my AI collaboration. Define what success and failure would look like before we build the test.

  10. Build a Minimum Viable DCPF Test

    Using the DCPF Introductory Edition as the authoritative source, guide me through building the smallest DCPF implementation appropriate for this test. Interview me for the project-specific decisions you need. Preserve all standard DCPF responsibilities, but do not add unnecessary structure merely for formality.

  11. Evaluate the Experiment

    After the test is complete, help me evaluate the results against the success criteria we established. Identify any problems by the DCPF responsibility most likely responsible: Governance, Research, Knowledge, or Runtime. Distinguish problems with DCPF itself from problems with our implementation, the AI model, the available evidence, or the test design. Do not approve changes or promote new information into Accepted Knowledge without my decision.

Evaluate first. Learn second. Test in a controlled environment. Decide from evidence.
